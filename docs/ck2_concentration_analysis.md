# CK2 — Job Concentration Trends and Case Studies

## Research Goal

We studied whether workplace jobs became less concentrated in the Bay Area after 2019.

We used LEHD LODES8 WAC data for 1,765 census tracts from 2017 to 2023.

## 1. Regional Concentration

We used two measures:

- Top 10% Share: The percentage of jobs in the top 10% of census tracts.
- HHI: A measure of how concentrated jobs are across census tracts.

### Results

- Top 10% Share: 59.46% (2019) to 59.75% (2023).
- HHI: 44.50 (2019) to 44.83 (2023).
- Both measures increased slightly.

### Interpretation

Our results do not show a clear decrease in workplace job concentration after 2019.

Overall, workplace job concentration remained relatively stable.

These results do not support our original prediction that workplace jobs became less concentrated after COVID.

## 2. Case Studies

We compared three approximate study areas:

| Area | 2019 Jobs | 2023 Jobs | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Downtown SF | 437,407 | 436,954 | -0.10% |
| Downtown San Jose | 47,834 | 48,090 | +0.54% |
| Dublin/Pleasanton | 77,773 | 78,027 | +0.33% |

### Interpretation

All three areas experienced declines in recorded workplace jobs after 2019, followed by recovery.

By 2023, workplace job totals were close to their 2019 levels.

## 3. Limitations

- WAC measures recorded workplace job locations, not actual daytime presence.
- The results do not directly measure remote work.
- Case study areas use approximate distance-based tract selections, not official downtown boundaries.
- Regional results may hide important local changes.
